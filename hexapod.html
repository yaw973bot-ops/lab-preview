WHY THESE PARTS?

Ball links are for articulated pushrods, not needed for these hinge pivots. For the first prototype, use a stationary M3 bolt as the axle through a 623ZZ bearing's nominal 3 mm inner bore. The bearing outer ring sits firmly in the moving 3D-printed link; the bolt passes through the stationary fork. On each side of the bearing, use a narrow printed sleeve that touches only the inner race. Tighten an M3 nut against the fork/sleeve stack without contacting the shields or outer race; the moving link must still spin freely. Normal M3 flat washers can be too wide to touch the bearing face directly. Measure actual screw/bearing fit first; nominal M3 does NOT guarantee ideal bore fit. Fully threaded bolts are acceptable for low-load prototype trials but not ideal precision bearing axles. This two-item goods basket is CA$24.90 before taxes/shipping; servos/electronics excluded.

BUILD ORDER

  1. 1Print M3 bolt clearance holes, a test pocket for the 623ZZ bearing (nominal 10 mm OD × 4 mm wide), and narrow printed inner-race spacer sleeves. Measure the bearing and bolt, and test the bolt through the bore without forcing.
  2. 2Measure the full joint stack (both fork sides + 4 mm bearing + both sleeve spacers + room for nut). Choose a 20 mm M3 bolt from the kit if long enough; otherwise source 25 mm or appropriate length. Capture the bearing outer race in the moving link. Clamp the inner-race spacer stack with a secure nut, then confirm no rubbing, binding or axial looseness before applying power.
  3. 3Select servo from measured size, horn, torque and speed; buy 3 + 1 spare.
  4. 4Power one leg; test travel, interference, load, flex and current. Improve before scaling to six.

ROBOT REQUIREMENTS & WEIGHT

Engineering notes · Draft targets · 14 Oct 2026

1 HOW ENGINEERS SET REQUIREMENTS

Start with the mission, not the weight: need → measurable target → estimate → prototype → test → revise. “Fast” must become a speed measured on a specified surface.

2 WORKING SYSTEM TARGETS

Speed: ≥0.20 m/s on flat indoor floor; stretch ≥0.40 m/s (time a 2 m run).

Mass: ≤1.50 kg assembled, battery included. Use 1.60 kg as a conservative mechanical load case—not the target weight.

Runtime: ≥15 min walking. Obstacle: clear a 20 mm threshold. Size: ~270 mm leg-tip span. All are provisional.

3 FIRST WEIGHT BUDGET

18 servos: 378 g | Battery: 130–200 g | Printed parts: 250–450 g

Pi + sensors: 70–120 g | Wiring + power + hardware: 120–220 g

TOTAL ≈ 948–1,368 g (0.95–1.37 kg). Update with real component and slicer weights.

4 WHY SET A MASS LIMIT EARLY?

Mass drives support force and servo torque. More battery can mean more mass, which can demand stronger motors and still more power. A mass budget helps stop this cycle; it is not the first or only requirement.

5 FIRST PHYSICS CHECK

At 1.60 kg on 3 supporting feet: static force/foot ≈ (1.60 × 9.81)/3 = 5.2 N. Walking loads can be higher. Joint torque depends on lever arm, angles and linkage transmission.

NEXT TESTS

Weigh components → print + load-test one leg → measure gait speed, current and runtime → refine servo, linkage and battery choices.
